Research

Our team studies how passively collected smartphone mobility data – step count, walking speed, step length, walking asymmetry, and double support time – can measure recovery after orthopaedic injury. The peer-reviewed papers below describe that work. Each is available to download, along with a link to the published version.

Preinjury Smartphone-Derived Mobility Metrics Are Associated With Discharge Location After Lower Extremity Fracture

Ian P. Marshall, Patrick J. McGlone, Janse T. Schermerhorn, Brian M. Shear, Dane J. Brodke, Robert V. O'Toole, Gerard P. Slobogean, Nathan N. O'Hara

JBJS Open Access. 2026;11:e26.00214. doi:10.2106/JBJS.OA.26.00214

In 249 adults with lower extremity fractures, preinjury step count, walking speed, and walking asymmetry recorded by Apple Health independently predicted whether patients went home or to a facility after hospital discharge. Objective preinjury mobility may improve risk stratification and discharge planning.

Impact of Weight-Bearing Status on Patient Gait During Fracture Recovery

Hammad Baqai, Prateek Swamykumar, Maggie Manchester, Patrick McGinnis, Patrick McGlone, Brian M. Shear, Dane J. Brodke, Gerard P. Slobogean, Robert V. O'Toole, Nathan N. O'Hara

Journal of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons. 2026;34(5):e692–e699. doi:10.5435/JAAOS-D-25-00075

Among 74 surgically treated lower extremity fracture patients, those allowed early weight-bearing took about 1,050 more steps per day in the first 12 weeks than those with delayed weight-bearing, and by 26 weeks walked faster with less asymmetry. Smartphone data can measure the effect of weight-bearing protocols continuously and remotely.

The Future Is Mobile: Pilot Validation Study of Apple Health Metrics in Orthopaedic Trauma

Dane J. Brodke, Brian M. Shear, Haley Demyanovich, Vivian Li, Alice Bell, David Okhuereigbe, Nelson F. SooHoo, Gerard P. Slobogean, Robert V. O'Toole, Nathan N. O'Hara

The Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery. 2025;107(16):1825–1832. doi:10.2106/JBJS.24.00842

In 107 patients after lower extremity fracture surgery, iPhone gait metrics captured a 93% drop in daily steps at injury, partial recovery by six months, moderate correlation with PROMIS physical function, and a slower step count recovery in patients who developed nonunion. These “digital biomarkers” show promise as objective, real-world outcome measures.

Predicting Post-Fracture Recovery with Smartphone Mobility Data: A Proof-of-Concept Study

Brian M. Shear, Dane J. Brodke, Gregory R. Hancock, Patrick McGlone, Haley Demyanovich, Vivian Li, Alice Bell, David Okhuereigbe, Gerard P. Slobogean, Robert V. O'Toole, Nathan N. O'Hara

The Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery. 2025;107(11):e57. doi:10.2106/JBJS.24.01305

Combining preinjury iPhone mobility data with demographic and injury characteristics produced well-calibrated models of post-fracture mobility over six months. Preinjury function strongly predicted recovery, and individualized recovery projections could support patient counseling and clinical decision-making.
